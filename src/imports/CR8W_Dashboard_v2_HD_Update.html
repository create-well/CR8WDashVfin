2. HD as a Tool, Not a Dictator

The dashboard doesn’t say “Sacral Authority” or “Ego Projected.” It says:
- “What’s your gut saying?” (not “Check your sacral”)
- “Bingle, what’s your read?” (the invitation, not the strategy)
- “Is this truly yours, or something you absorbed from the room?” (the absorption check, not “open chart conditioning”)
- “Has Omar checked the gear?” (the Line 1 security need, not the “Investigator profile”)

The HD data lives in the CO-FLOW tab as the How We Flow reference. It is available when the team wants it. It is not required reading before using the dashboard.

B. The Absorption Check — A New Somatic Entry Point

Before the mood check-in, a subtle prompt appears:

Before you drop in: is anything you're carrying not yours?
[ I'm clear ]  [ The room feels heavy ]  [ Not sure — check in first ]

Why this matters: Bingle and Omar have the most open charts — they absorb the most from the environment. Monny’s open Heart means she may overcommit to prove worth. Sunshine’s open Heart/Spleen means she may pick up others’ fears as her own intuition. This is a 10-second check that saves 20 minutes of off-flow conversation later.

The dashboard logs this (anonymously) and shows a team pattern: “Three of you felt the room was heavy this week. What’s the water showing you?”

The Topic Well flow:
1. Phase 1: The Drop (Monday, async) — Each person drops a “CREATING AND _____” fill. No pressure. No obligation. Anonymous or named.
2. Phase 2: The Synthesis (Monny, Monday-Tuesday) — Monny reads the drops, finds the pattern, proposes 2-3 candidates with her gut response (mmm-hmm / unh-unh). She is the synthesizer. She creates the “flow motion” and “flowhoe pathways.”
3. Phase 3: The Flow Keeper’s Call (Monday late / Tuesday) — The Flow Keeper reviews Monny’s synthesis and locks the topic based on their gut. Not a vote. A response.
4. Phase 4: The Lock (Tuesday, by end of day) — The topic is confirmed. The Question Bank auto-filters. The Weekly Rhythm Strip updates. The Role Banner shows the topic. Omar receives the topic so his Sacral can respond to the technical needs.

HD safety in the Topic Well:
- Sunshine (MG): initiates the template. But must wait through her emotional wave before locking.
- Monny (Generator): responds to the drops. Her synthesis is a bridge, not a creation. Her gut is the filter. Her open Heart means she may over-synthesize to prove worth.
- Bingle (Projector): waits for invitation to drop. Her taste is the filter. “Bingle, what does your taste tell you about Monny’s synthesis?”
- Omar (MG): responds to the real problem. “Omar, what’s the gear teaching you about creating this week?”

The language: The Topic Well speaks the team’s actual voice: “drop it in,” “the well,” “flow motion,” “flowhoe pathways,” “explwhoreatory,” “my gut said yes,” “the water is rough,” “mmm-hmm / unh-unh,” “toss in topics,” “feel freeeeeeeness.”

Updated Design Principles

  1. Flow over force. Every element earns its place. If it doesn’t serve a weekly decision, it goes.
  2. The banks hold the water. The dashboard makes the operating rhythm visible — not just possible.
  3. Four users, one view. Sunshine, Monny, Bingle, and Omar each see what they need without switching contexts.
  4. HD is a tool, not a language. The dashboard speaks Create Well. The HD data lives in the reference doc.
  5. Live data or nothing. A stat that reads 0 is worse than no stat. Connect it or hide it.
  6. Metaphor is functional. “The Well” isn’t just a name. It’s where drops become knowledge.
  7. Omar is visible. The gear card, the shortcut, the technical status — his contribution is as visible as the creative work.
  8. The team’s voice is the interface. “Drop it in.” “Feel freeeeeeeness.” “Explwhoreatory.” The dashboard speaks like the team actually talks.
  9. The Topic Well is the creative engine. Sunshine’s template. Monny’s synthesis. The Flow Keeper’s gut. The team chooses what flows.
